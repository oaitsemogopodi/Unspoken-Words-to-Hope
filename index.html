Where I Fell Short

I see where I failed. I see the moments I chose my own understanding over patience, my own fears over faith, my own rush over God's timing.

I have reflected deeply, and I acknowledge the hurt I caused. There are no excuses, no blaming—only the sober recognition that love requires more than good intentions; it requires daily surrender, humility, and selflessness.

I am sorry for the times I didn't listen well enough, for the moments I let pride speak louder than grace, for the instances where I was more focused on being right than being kind.

This is repentance, not guilt. This is the starting point of change.

Choosing God, Choosing Growth

I want to do things God's way this time. Not in my strength, but in His. Not on my timeline, but on His.

This means rebuilding with prayer as the foundation, with patience as the process, and with obedience as the path. Love is not rushed—it is stewarded. It is nurtured in quiet moments of seeking, in daily decisions to die to self, in consistent choices to honor God first.

I am learning that true love protects, trusts, hopes, and perseveres—and it always points back to Christ.

If there is a future for us, I want it to be one where God is at the center, where our relationship is an overflow of our individual walks with Him.

A Second Chance, Not a Forced One

I am asking for a chance to do better—not demanding, not assuming, just asking with an open and surrendered heart.

I acknowledge your feelings, your need for space, your right to peace. Love does not force; love respects. Love does not manipulate; love waits.

If you choose to open your heart again, I promise to move slowly, to listen intentionally, to love patiently. If you need time, I will honor that. If you need silence, I will hold my prayers quietly.

This is about your healing as much as it is about my growth. Your peace matters more than my desire.

The Future I Still See

I still see a future where we grow together—not a perfect one, but a real one. One where we pray together, dream together, and build a life anchored in wisdom and faith.

I see morning prayers shared over coffee, honest conversations about fears and hopes, and a home filled with grace rather than perfection.

I see us healing old wounds, learning new rhythms of grace, and creating space for each other to become who God designed us to be.

This is not a fantasy—it is a hopeful, prayerful vision of what could be if God wills it and if our hearts align in His timing.

A Letter From My Heart

Hey Hope,

I've been thinking about you lately. Not in an obsessive way, but in that quiet way you think about someone who really mattered to you. You know, when something funny happens and you think "Hope would laugh at this" or when you see a beautiful sunset and remember how much you appreciated them.

I miss your laugh. The real one—not the polite one, but the one where you'd forget anyone was watching and just fully enjoy a moment. I miss our conversations where time would just disappear. The easy ones, where we'd talk about nothing and everything.

I want you to know—and I mean this in the simplest, truest way—that you're a really good person. The kind of person who makes people around them feel seen and heard. I don't know if I ever properly told you that.

If I'm honest, I'm working on myself. Not to impress you or win you back or anything like that. Just... because being around you made me realize I could be better. Kinder. More present. More patient.

I don't have any grand expectations here. Life is messy and complicated, and I get that. Whatever you're feeling or needing right now—space, time, distance—I respect that completely. Your peace matters.

I just wanted to say: I see you. I appreciate who you are. And whatever path you choose, I hope it brings you happiness. I really mean that.

No pressure. No drama. Just wanted to put that out there.

Thinking of you,
Mr M
